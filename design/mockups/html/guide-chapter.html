Shapes

Rectangles, circles, ellipses, lines and points, and where each one is measured from. Run the sketch, then change a number and watch where the shape goes.

def draw():    f.background("white")    f.fill("skyblue")    f.rect(40, 60, 160, 100)      # x, y, width, height    f.fill("tomato")    f.circle(320, 110, 100)       # centre x, y, diameter    f.fill("gold")    f.ellipse(520, 110, 160, 80)  # centre, width, height    f.stroke("navy")    f.stroke_width(4)    f.line(40, 260, 600, 340)
shapes/01_basic_shapes.py · whole file Copy Run it here
FunctionPlaced by
f.rect(x, y, w, h)its top-left corner
f.circle(x, y, d)its centre; d is the diameter, not the radius
f.ellipse(x, y, w, h)its centre
f.line(x1, y1, x2, y2)its two ends; uses the stroke colour only
f.point(x, y)a dot, as wide as the stroke

Coordinates can have decimals, so f.circle(100.5, 80.25, 40) is fine, and edges are smoothed.

Try thisChange 100 in f.circle to 200. Which way does the circle grow? Now change the 40 in f.rect.

More shapes

Squares, triangles, quads, polygons and arcs follow the same idea: a few numbers say where the corners or the centre are. The picture under each example shows exactly what the numbers do.

FunctionNotes
f.square(x, y, size)Placed by its top-left corner, like rect
f.triangle(...), f.quad(...)Corners in order; the shape is closed and filled
f.polygon(points)A list of (x, y) points; closed and filled
f.arc(x, y, w, h, start, stop, mode)Part of an ellipse. Angles are in degrees, starting at 3 o'clock and turning clockwise

Placing shapes by their centre or corners

A rectangle is placed by its top-left corner and a circle by its centre. When you would rather place a rectangle by its centre, say so once with f.rect_mode("center"), and every rectangle after it is placed that way.
